# Personalization without exceptionalism

Personal response requires repeated evidence.

## Copying and nostalgia {#individual-errors-1}

Doing exactly a partner’s workout ignores different capacity and response. Copying an elite routine adds differences in training age, workload tolerance and recovery conditions. Repeating an old personal routine ignores the possibility that the same lifter has changed. Internet templates need interpretation rather than automatic rejection or blind obedience.

The authors illustrate that an otherwise useful structure can work after a reduction in sets or a more specific exercise substitution. Those examples identify a principle-level mismatch. They do not show that every famous template is optimal once any arbitrary adjustment is made.

## No one is exempt from the foundations {#individual-errors-2}

“This works best for me” should be supported by repeated outcomes, not preference alone. Individuality cannot justify training with no relevant stimulus, uncontrolled chronic fatigue or technique that fails the target task. Preferences matter because adherence matters, but they should be distinguished from demonstrated performance effects.

Likewise, one disappointing program does not prove its entire structure is wrong. Was the dose too high? Was the exercise selection poorly matched? Was recovery unusually constrained? The chapter’s central claim is that people differ in meaningful but usually manageable ways while sharing the same basic training requirements.

## Recap

- Personal response requires repeated evidence.
- Adjusting a dose is different from abandoning a principle.
