# Carry gains into the next phase

Every phase develops something and preserves something else.

## Development and preservation goals {#phase-transitions-1}

Every phase has both an adaptation goal and preservation goals. In a hypertrophy phase, muscle and work capacity dominate while the lifter retains enough connection to forceful lifting. In a strength phase, developing force dominates while enough work preserves muscle. In peaking, maximal-load execution dominates while size and general strength are maintained as far as practical.

The authors’ typical sets and loading ranges are broad planning aids, not additive prescriptions to perform all blocks at once. A small amount of supporting work can smooth transitions. The source favors a clear majority devoted to the phase goal, while its example 70/30 split is a coaching heuristic rather than an experimentally established cutoff.

> Scientific Principles of Strength Training, pp. 289–292, 300–307 (printed page labels; PDF page index is label minus 1).

## Protect newly developed capacities {#phase-transitions-2}

If a weak quadriceps group receives focused development, abruptly removing every quad-biased exercise may make its gains harder to retain. A related compound movement in the next phase can provide continuity. Similarly, triceps development from an isolation movement can be carried forward through an appropriate pressing variant rather than switching entirely to a different emphasis.

Too-long hypertrophy blocks can encounter diminishing returns; too-long strength blocks may exhaust improvements available from current muscle size; too-long peaks can sacrifice the base. Changing emphasis every few days creates the opposite problem. The correct question is whether the current phase is still producing its intended benefit and preparing what follows.

> Scientific Principles of Strength Training, pp. 289–292, 300–307 (printed page labels; PDF page index is label minus 1).

## Recap

- Every phase develops something and preserves something else.
- Transitions should retain newly gained but vulnerable capacities.
