# Different goals, different overloads

Source loading ranges are contextual coaching recommendations.

## Interpret the book’s loading ranges {#goal-specific-overload-1}

The authors’ practical framework uses roughly 60% 1RM and above for powerlifting-oriented hypertrophy, about 75% and above for basic strength, and above 85% for near-maximal technical practice. These are the source’s coaching ranges, not universal biological cutoffs. The book’s claim that lighter training is largely inappropriate for hypertrophy should not be generalized into an absolute rule about all resistance training.

Within its framework, hypertrophy puts a premium on sufficient recoverable volume, strength on substantial force exposure with moderate volume, and peaking on low-volume practice of very heavy regulation lifts. A block’s goal determines which variable deserves emphasis.

> Scientific Principles of Strength Training, pp. 78–86 (printed page labels; PDF page index is label minus 1).

## A numerical comparison {#goal-specific-overload-2}

An original arithmetic example: 4 × 8 × 100 kg equals 3,200 kg·repetitions; 3 × 5 × 110 kg equals 1,650 kg·repetitions. The second session is heavier but has less volume load. It may fit a transition toward strength, but it cannot be described as increased volume simply because the bar is heavier.

For peaking, small errors in setup, walkout or bracing become more consequential when there is little force reserve. Heavy practice provides task-specific information that light repetitions cannot fully replicate. Its fatigue cost also limits total work. The final taper intentionally reduces overload; preserving fitness while removing fatigue becomes the immediate objective.

## Recap

- Source loading ranges are contextual coaching recommendations.
- A heavier session can have lower volume load.
