# Cardio, mobility and competing goals

State the goal before evaluating an activity’s cost.

## Avoid both extremes {#cardio-mobility-1}

The source rejects both “cardio guarantees better lifting” and “any cardio destroys gains.” A basic capacity to tolerate training can be useful, especially during higher-repetition work. Large additional endurance demands can compete with the recoverable workload available for strength training. The authors prefer less disruptive modes and placing dedicated work where it least conflicts with the main phase.

Its numerical calorie ceiling and body-fat examples are author-specific estimates, not universal health guidance. The course does not turn them into automatic prescriptions. Improving recovery feelings is not by itself proof that long-term strength adaptation improved; assess both the desired performance and the other goals the athlete values.

## Enough range for the task {#cardio-mobility-2}

Mobility work should address a demonstrated restriction in range, strength or technique. Once the required positions can be used reliably, endlessly expanding flexibility can consume time without solving a performance constraint. The source discusses acute stretching effects and cautions against large disruptive doses before demanding lifting; effects depend on the actual intervention and context.

Similarly, training several sports can be a valid choice, especially for enjoyment or broader development. It does not follow that every goal can be maximized simultaneously. The book’s priority is maximal powerlifting performance; a learner with broader priorities may intentionally accept a different tradeoff. State the objective before judging the plan.

## Recap

- State the goal before evaluating an activity’s cost.
- Basic capacity can help without making unlimited additional work useful.
