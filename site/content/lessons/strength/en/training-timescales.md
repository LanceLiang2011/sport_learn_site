# From a session to a macrocycle

Planning units are defined by training purpose.

## Nested planning units {#training-timescales-1}

A session is one bout with a beginning and end. A microcycle spans the recurrence of a session type, often a week but not necessarily seven days. An accumulation phase comprises progressively demanding microcycles. A deload is a microcycle of substantially easier work intended to reduce fatigue while retaining adaptations.

A mesocycle organizes microcycles toward a distinct adaptation, often accumulation followed by deload. A block groups one or more mesocycles sharing a purpose. A macrocycle orders blocks toward a larger outcome, often a competition. These units describe function; calendar length alone does not determine the unit.

> Scientific Principles of Strength Training, pp. 14–18 (printed page labels; PDF page index is label minus 1).

## Training and nutrition are different axes {#training-timescales-2}

Hypertrophy, basic strength and peaking name training goals. Mass gain, maintenance and cutting name body-mass or nutritional goals. They interact but are not synonyms. A hypertrophy-style block can be used to retain muscle during a cut; a maintenance diet can accompany several training goals.

An illustrative macrocycle is muscle development → strength development → peaking → competition → active rest. Active rest deliberately lacks an accumulation phase. A 3:1 accumulation-to-deload ratio means three units of accumulation for one of deload, not that every athlete must use four-week months. All lengths must remain compatible with recovery and the competition calendar.

> Scientific Principles of Strength Training, pp. 14–18 (printed page labels; PDF page index is label minus 1).

## Planning and measurement map {#timescale-map}

| Quantity | What to record | A common mistaken inference |
|---|---|---|
| External load | kg or lb, including the bar | A heavier bar always means a harder set |
| Failure proximity | Repetitions still possible under the stated conditions | The same %1RM means the same effort |
| Volume load | Sets × repetitions × kg | Equal tonnage means equal adaptation across lifts |
| Frequency | Exposures per named interval | More sessions always means more weekly work |
| Microcycle | Recurrence of a session pattern | Every microcycle must be exactly seven days |
| Block goal | Development plus preservation | Nutrition and training phase names are interchangeable |

A hypothetical lifter logs 100 kg × 8 with two repetitions available, then later 102.5 kg × 8 with the same reserve and range. That is interpretable load progression. If the second set instead uses shortened range or reaches failure, several variables changed. Record that difference before comparing. The purpose of measurement is to preserve the meaning of the comparison, not merely collect a larger number.

> Scientific Principles of Strength Training, pp. 12–20 (printed page labels and 1-based PDF pages coincide; 0-based PDF index is label minus 1).

## Recap

- Planning units are defined by training purpose.
- Nutritional phases and training blocks should not be confused.
