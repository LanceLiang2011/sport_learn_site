# Give an adaptation time to develop

A dominant emphasis can coexist with moderate variation.

## Concentrated emphasis {#directed-adaptation-1}

Directed adaptation means presenting related stimuli consistently enough for a capacity to develop and persist. One isolated exposure to a repetition range is not equivalent to weeks of organized practice. The authors argue that repeatedly switching among conflicting goals can dilute the best development of any one goal. This is a case for a dominant emphasis, not a ban on all within-week variety.

For example, a general-strength phase can include modest differences in repetitions while keeping its main purpose stable. An assistance exercise should be selected for a muscle, movement or technical need and repeated long enough to assess its response. Changing every session makes it difficult to tell learning effects from durable improvement.

## Use the right tool at the right distance {#directed-adaptation-2}

Early in a macrocycle, a weak muscle may justify substantial assistance work. Near a meet, the same work can take recovery away from regulation lifts. Practicing competition standards also includes the required range and controlled execution; a stronger movement that no longer meets the standard does not demonstrate improved competition performance.

The book challenges non-directed general preparation that merely makes a lifter tired. An activity should build a required capacity, address a real limitation or be included for another explicitly valued goal. If another sport matters too, acknowledge the mixed objective rather than pretending the program solely maximizes powerlifting.

## Recap

- A dominant emphasis can coexist with moderate variation.
- Assistance work must serve a need and match the training phase.
