# Too little and too much specificity

Year-round readiness can displace long-term development.

## Why not maximal singles all year? {#specificity-errors-1}

Constant competition-heavy training improves readiness to display strength but may leave insufficient sustainable volume to build muscle and general strength. The authors also identify staleness and the opportunity cost of always being nearly competition-ready. High specificity is valuable when performance must be expressed; it is not the only useful training state.

At the other extreme, an exercise chosen only because it is different may lack transfer. Directed variation sits between these extremes: a front squat can emphasize quadriceps, and a close-grip press can increase a triceps contribution, provided execution actually retains that emphasis. Recreating the same dominant pattern in the variant can defeat its purpose.

## A practical transfer audit {#specificity-errors-2}

For each exercise, write four short answers: target capacity, expected transfer, current phase and cost. An illustrative answer might be: “Develop knee-extensor capacity; support the squat; early preparation; moderate additional leg work while monitoring trunk fatigue.” The numbers and outcome still need observation; the statement is a testable rationale, not proof.

The chapter’s further reading addresses training transfer, regional hypertrophy, concurrent training, fatigue and stretching.
