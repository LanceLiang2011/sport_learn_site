# Capacity, technique and mobility

Task-specific performance depends on the limiting portion.

## General capacity and the limiting point {#strength-mobility-1}

Strength is maximal force-producing capacity. Task-specific strength is that capacity expressed in a particular movement under its constraints. A deadlift that can be locked out from blocks with 250 kg but can only be initiated from the floor with 200 kg is limited to a 200 kg full lift. Partial-range ability does not establish full-range competition ability.

Technique organizes positions and movement to use available force effectively. Body proportions alter feasible positions, but individualization does not remove the need to satisfy competition standards or maintain controlled execution. Practice must address the actual limiting portion rather than only the strongest portion.

## Mobility is more than stretching {#strength-mobility-2}

The book defines mobility as the intersection of flexibility, strength and technique. Flexibility permits a range of motion; strength supports positions; technique supplies the coordination and knowledge to use them. A lifter who can reach squat depth unloaded but cannot maintain position under a manageable load may not have a flexibility-only problem.

Use an assessment question rather than an automatic intervention: Is the missing position unavailable, unsupported, or not yet learned? This model is a conceptual distinction, not a diagnosis.
