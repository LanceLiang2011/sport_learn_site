# Fit the plan to the lifter

Individual technique differs within sound execution and task constraints.

## Exercise and technique choices {#technique-selection-1}

Limb proportions and joint structure influence useful positions and leverages. Two technically sound squats need not look identical. The authors retain common foundations such as controlled execution and bracing while allowing differences in stance, grip or movement strategy. This conceptual freedom does not replace direct coaching or the need to satisfy the competition standard.

A useful exercise is one that a lifter can execute reliably, progressively load and connect to a real goal. Another athlete’s preferred stance or assistance lift is only a candidate. Evaluate comfort, controllability, performance and the effect on the target lift rather than copying a photograph.

## Change one important variable {#technique-selection-2}

Start from a reasonable general plan and identify the specific mismatch. If the structure works but the total dose is unrecoverable, adjusting set counts may be more informative than replacing every exercise and frequency at once. If assistance has poor transfer, change its selection while keeping enough other factors stable to interpret the result.

The book favors progression based on the individual’s observed responses. A log can record the intended change, a comparable performance measure and a review date. This creates a small programming experiment. It does not guarantee causal certainty, but it is stronger evidence than a single good or bad day attached to a program’s name.

## Recap

- Individual technique differs within sound execution and task constraints.
- Target a specific mismatch before replacing an entire plan.
