# Sufficient now, progressive over time

Overload combines a meaningful stimulus with long-term progression.

## Two conditions {#overload-conditions-1}

Overload has two parts in this book: the stimulus must be sufficiently challenging to produce a meaningful adaptation, and average demands must grow beyond recent experience as adaptation occurs. A load can be new without being relevant, and a relevant load can become too easy over time. Specificity therefore precedes overload.

Progression can use load, repetitions, sets or a combination, provided it preserves the intended training effect. The comparison window is longer than one consecutive session. Planned light sessions and deloads are compatible with progressive overload because they make later productive work possible. A program that demands a personal record every session confuses local difficulty with sustainable progression.

## MRV is contextual {#overload-conditions-2}

Maximum recoverable volume (MRV) is the highest workload that can be recovered from sufficiently to benefit. It is a changing estimate, not a number calculated from body mass. Exercise, load, failure proximity, nutrition, sleep and the comparison time frame all matter. The authors advocate working near this boundary when maximizing results is the goal, while recognizing that exceeding it adds cost without proportionate benefit.

Track standardized performance across repeated exposures and interpret it with recovery context. A persistent fall in attainable repetitions at comparable conditions can flag an excessive dose, but one poor session is not a precise MRV measurement. Log the conditions and look for patterns before claiming a stable limit.
