# Load, effort and work

Load and effort are separate variables.

## Three numbers, three meanings {#training-variables-1}

The book uses [intensity](term:intensity) for the external resistance, commonly reported as the mass on the bar in kg or lb. Its term **relative intensity** means proximity to concentric failure. This differs from the convention that calls percentage of one-repetition maximum “relative intensity.” Always name the convention in a training log. A heavy single with several repetitions available can use more load but less effort than a lighter set taken near failure.

**Frequency** counts training exposures per stated time unit. “Three sessions per week” does not specify their exercises, difficulty or distribution. Exercise selection identifies the actual movement; “legs” is not a reproducible exercise prescription.

## Volume is a comparison tool {#training-variables-2}

For repeated identical lifts, the book uses volume load as a convenient proxy:

**Volume load = sets × repetitions × load (kg·repetitions).**

This is not mechanical work in joules. Work also requires displacement and force; kilograms describe mass, not force. If the same lifter performs 4 × 5 × 100 kg, the proxy is 2,000 kg·repetitions. A different range of motion, exercise, machine leverage or lifter changes its meaning. Thus equal tonnage in curls and deadlifts does not imply equal stimulus or fatigue. Record exercise, range, effort and rest alongside volume; compare like with like.

## Recap

- Load and effort are separate variables.
- Volume load omits displacement and is meaningful mainly within comparable lifts.
