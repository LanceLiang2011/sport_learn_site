# A spectrum of transfer

Specificity is a spectrum, not visual imitation.

## From muscle to competition lift {#specificity-spectrum-1}

Specificity means training the systems responsible for the target task. Growing the muscles used in a squat is broadly specific; developing their force output is narrower; executing a regulation squat near the competition load is narrower still. A supporting exercise need not visually match the competition movement to contribute, but its mechanism of transfer should be explainable.

The original specificity tree links muscle development to several sports. Shared foundational capacity does not make two sports identical. A program designed to maximize a powerlifting total should increasingly emphasize the actual squat, bench and deadlift as competition approaches, while earlier work may emphasize their underlying capacities.

## Compatibility is a resource question {#specificity-spectrum-2}

The book discusses strength with endurance, flexibility, power, hypertrophy and task-specific strength. Modalities can support one another yet compete for time, recoverable workload and the physiological state needed for the next session. Its strongest criticism concerns large endurance commitments when maximal powerlifting performance is the sole priority; this is not a statement that all cardiovascular activity is harmful.

Strength requires high force; power combines force with velocity. Maximal intent during a lift does not mean a near-maximal bar will move quickly. Flexibility should permit the needed positions, while additional flexibility work has to justify its recovery and time cost. These are goal-dependent tradeoffs, not universal rankings of exercise value.

## Mechanisms of transfer and interference {#compatibility-map}

| Pairing | Potential support | Potential conflict in the source |
|---|---|---|
| Strength + endurance | Enough general capacity to tolerate training | High additional volume, fatigue and competing adaptations |
| Strength + flexibility | Access to required positions | Unneeded range work costs time; acute effects depend on dose and timing |
| Strength + power | Shared forceful intent | Very light velocity emphasis may lack the target force stimulus |
| Strength + hypertrophy | More muscle can support later force | High-volume fatigue can impair immediate heavy execution |
| General + specific strength | General capacity supports the competition lift | Maximal expression needs more exact skill and lower fatigue |

The authors explain increasing competition specificity through muscle architecture, connective-tissue loading lines and neural coordination. Motor units within a muscle, contributions among muscles and the timing of the complete movement can all differ between variants. Their practical conclusion is to move toward the actual competition technique in time to adapt, rather than discovering the difference during the heaviest final weeks.

For example, touch-and-go bench performance does not establish paused bench performance. Using the first to prescribe the second can overestimate the available repetitions. Likewise, removing straps from a deadlift changes a constraint. External training has four contextual dimensions in the source: volume, impact/disruption, modality and timing. A low-dose separate hobby differs from a high-dose activity immediately before heavy lifting. GPP still needs a relationship to the sport: it prepares the capacities needed for later specific training, rather than merely producing exhaustion.

## Recap

- Specificity is a spectrum, not visual imitation.
- Evaluate transfer and the recovery cost together.
