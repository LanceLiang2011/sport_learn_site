# Compare the four blocks

Tables summarize principles and do not replace individual observation.

## A map of the macrocycle {#block-comparison-1}

The source’s summary tables consolidate the earlier chapters. Read them as a decision aid rather than an automatic personalized program. Its typical weekly working-set examples are approximately 15–30 per muscle group in hypertrophy, 10–20 in strength and 5–10 overloading sets in peaking, with possible light work. These are the book’s ranges for its context; overlapping compound contributions make naive set addition misleading.

| Block | Main outcome | Main emphasis | Preserve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypertrophy | Muscle and work capacity | Relevant variants, more volume | Connection to strength |
| Basic strength | General force capacity | Heavy compounds, moderate volume | Muscle |
| Peaking | Competition 1RM expression | Regulation lifts, low volume | Muscle and general strength |
| Active rest | Readiness for a new cycle | Low volume and loading | Enough practice and capacity |

## Check the denominator {#block-comparison-2}

The tables mix minimum loading, average loading, overloading sessions and light sessions. An average of heavy and light days is not the minimum load used on every day. Similarly, a percentage of a previous overload day is not automatically a percentage of 1RM. Label the reference explicitly before calculating.

Some recommendations differ between the narrative and the summary. For example, the narrative’s first-half strength deload example differs from the summary table. Rather than selecting an apparently exact universal answer, retain the common principle: meaningfully reduce the relevant demands while preserving enough stimulus. The course intentionally flags such source variation instead of hiding it.
