# Build, strengthen, express

Sequence matters only if previous gains are retained.

## Why sequence can help {#phase-sequence-1}

[Phase potentiation](term:phase-potentiation) organizes training so an earlier adaptation supports a later one. The authors’ central powerlifting sequence is muscle development → basic strength → peaking. More muscle provides material for later force development; general strength provides capacity for task-specific maximal performance. Peaking comes last because it prepares the expression required on competition day.

The argument combines specificity, directed adaptation, compatibility and sequence. It does not claim other methods cannot work. The authors rank phase potentiation below specificity, overload and fatigue management, and describe its added benefit as relatively modest. A sophisticated phase diagram cannot rescue training that lacks an adequate relevant stimulus.

## Adaptive decay creates constraints {#phase-sequence-2}

A phase can potentiate the next only if the useful adaptation persists. Maintenance generally requires less stimulus than development, but not no stimulus. An excessively long low-volume peak may allow muscle or general strength to decline. Conversely, changing phases before meaningful adaptation develops can prevent any durable base.

The source gives very broad possible durations—roughly three weeks to six months for hypertrophy or strength, and three weeks to three months for peaking. These are upper and lower coaching bounds, not a recommendation that all learners spend that long in each phase. Competition timing and observed response decide what is appropriate.
