# Fitness and fatigue travel together

Fatigue has several contributors and time scales.

## Four categories, several time scales {#fatigue-systems-1}

The book groups cumulative fatigue into fuel stores, nervous-system disruption, chemical messengers and tissue structure. Glycogen supports repeated demanding work; the phosphagen system recovers much faster between efforts. Neural fatigue can affect force production, coordination and learning. Chemical signaling and damage to muscle or connective tissue may operate over longer time scales.

This is the authors’ explanatory model. It does not permit identifying a specific neurotransmitter deficit or hormone state from feeling tired. Mechanistic descriptions of mTOR, AMPK, testosterone or cortisol are simplified and should not be used as a home diagnostic test. Performance, context and repeated observations are the practical programming evidence.

## Overreaching is not a badge {#fatigue-systems-2}

The source distinguishes normal recoverable training, short-term overreaching and more persistent overtraining. Planned functional overreaching is an intentional temporary mismatch between stimulus and recovery followed by enough easier training to realize a benefit. Insufficient sleep can create a recovery mismatch without adding any useful training stimulus.

The authors use some overtraining labels differently from standardized clinical usage. Here the actionable distinction is duration and consequence: a planned short dip followed by recovery differs from sustained regression. A poor session alone establishes neither state. Accumulated fatigue must eventually be reduced; continuously demanding more work while performance deteriorates defeats the reason for training.

## Recap

- Fatigue has several contributors and time scales.
- Do not infer biochemical diagnoses from subjective fatigue.
