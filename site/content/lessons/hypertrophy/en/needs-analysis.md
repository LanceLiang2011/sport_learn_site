# Build a needs analysis

Turn preferences and physique demands into explicit priorities.

## Needs, strengths and weaknesses {#needs-analysis-priorities}

First describe the desired outcome. Competitive physique standards and a hobbyist’s personal preferences need not be the same. Then compare current strengths and weaknesses with that outcome and rank muscle groups by priority. A program that always trains a lagging muscle last may not match its stated priorities.

Allocate more fresh training time to priority muscles while preserving enough work elsewhere. Beginners generally have room to train the whole body productively; advanced lifters may need specialization. Avoid prescribing advanced specialization merely because a novice dislikes one body part. The practical question is what limits useful progress, not which routine looks most professional.

> Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training, ch. 1, EPUB Text/part0004.xhtml, Needs analysis in physique training

## Compounds, isolations and nutrition {#needs-analysis-tools}

Compound movements stimulate several muscles efficiently, while isolations can add local work when a synergist or systemic fatigue limits compounds. Neither class is universally superior. Excessively narrow exercise selection can miss regional or biomechanical actions and concentrate repetitive stress; excessive isolation can waste time and broader stimulus opportunities.

Diet supports the training goal. The source discusses adequate protein at approximately **1 g per lb of body mass per day** (about **2.2 g/kg/day**) and sufficient energy for growth. This is the authors’ general nutrition context, not a personal dietary prescription. Growth, maintenance and fat-loss phases create different recovery conditions; later chapters return to those differences.

> Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training, ch. 1, EPUB Text/part0004.xhtml, The Critical Role of Diet in Hypertrophy; Over-Application of Specificity

## An original priority map {#needs-analysis-allocation-map}

| Needs-analysis finding | Program implication | Check in the log |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A muscle is a genuine priority | Give it fresh attention and appropriate dose | Does it always end up last? |
| A muscle is already developed enough | Preserve it with an appropriate maintenance emphasis | Is extra work consuming recovery? |
| Several goals matter | State a current block priority | Is the promised outcome realistic? |
| A compound is limited by a support muscle | Adjust technique, exercise mix or complementary isolation | Which structure actually ends the set? |

This original decision table combines the source’s needs analysis and under/over-application examples; it is not an automatic prescription. The hamstrings illustrate complementary actions: knee flexion and hip extension are not trained identically by leg curls and hip hinges. Likewise, a press and a fly do not cover precisely the same pectoral action or range.

The nutrition discussion additionally proposes 4–6 reasonably spaced meals with daily protein spread fairly evenly. This is a source-specific example supporting the broader point that diet can change the result of the same program. It is not a necessary meal schedule for every learner.

> Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training, ch. 1, EPUB Text/part0004.xhtml, Needs analysis; The Critical Role of Diet in Hypertrophy; Multiple muscular biomechanical actions and ranges of motion exist

## Recap

- Make muscle priorities explicit before choosing exercises.
- Use compound and isolation movements as complementary tools.
