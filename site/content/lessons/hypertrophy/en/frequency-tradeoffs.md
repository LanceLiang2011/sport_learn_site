# Why both extremes can fail

More frequency solves some problems and creates others.

## Too infrequent {#frequency-tradeoffs-low}

Large infrequent sessions can produce excessive local damage, declining technique, weaker muscle awareness and systemic fatigue. Glycogen depletion and excitation–contraction limitations can further reduce later-set quality. More time between sessions is not a complete solution if each session creates unnecessarily large damage or if productive growth-related time is left unused.

The source discusses a likely useful region around 3–8 sets per muscle per session and often 2–4 weekly exposures, with broad individual exceptions. These are starting ideas for trained lifters, not universal minimums or maximums. Study populations, failure definitions and whether weekly volume was equated affect interpretation.

> Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training, ch. 4, EPUB Text/part0013.xhtml, Excessively Low Frequencies; The Current Research on Hypertrophy Frequency

## Too frequent {#frequency-tradeoffs-high}

Tiny highly frequent sessions repeat warm-ups and may lose the benefit of several sets for technical familiarity, target-muscle awareness and metabolite accumulation. Joint and connective tissues can recover more slowly than muscle; a frequency tolerated by muscle alone may therefore be unsustainable over months.

Very frequent training can also raise weekly work until systemic recovery fails. Individual factors include muscle size, strength, fiber distribution, architecture, sex and the actual exercise used. Average group differences do not determine an individual’s schedule. Supporting muscles count too: spinal erectors, elbows or shoulder structures may be loaded across many nominally different muscle days.

> Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training, ch. 4, EPUB Text/part0013.xhtml, Excessively High Frequencies; Intrinsic Variables that Affect Best Frequency Choices; Supporting Muscle Group Overuse

## Recap

- Neither maximum rest nor maximum frequency is automatically best.
- Track muscle, connective-tissue and systemic recovery together.
