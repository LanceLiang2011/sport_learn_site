# Load, effort and volume landmarks

Understand useful ranges without turning them into universal prescriptions.

## Read load and effort separately {#effective-ranges-load-effort}

Absolute load is the resistance lifted. Relative load expresses it as a fraction of that exercise’s 1RM. Relative effort is expressed here as [repetitions in reserve](term:rir) (RIR): an estimate of how many additional full, technically acceptable repetitions remain. A set of ten can be easy or near failure depending on the person and load.

The book describes a broad working range of approximately **30–85% 1RM** when sets are close enough to failure, usually **5–0 RIR**. These are source ranges with assumptions, not precise cutoffs. Very light loads require many repetitions and can be limited by discomfort; very heavy loads constrain total work and raise fatigue costs. Average effort around 2–3 RIR is an author guideline, not a prediction of growth.

## Four volume landmarks {#effective-ranges-landmarks}

| Landmark | Meaning in the source framework |
| --- | --- |
| MV | Enough training to maintain current muscle |
| MEV | Enough to begin robust measurable growth |
| [MAV](term:mav) | The currently most productive volume region |
| MRV | The greatest volume from which recovery permits continued productive training |

These are individual, muscle-specific, phase-dependent estimates. MAV is not a fixed set target to chase forever. The authors often organize accumulation from near MEV toward MRV, followed by reduced training. Continuing to add sets after recovery and performance fail is not productive overload.

## Hard sets versus tonnage {#effective-ranges-measurement}

The source usually counts hard sets within useful load and effort ranges to compare hypertrophy volume. Tonnage is **sets × repetitions × load**, with units such as kg; it omits displacement and is not mechanical work. Work would require force and distance, with units of joules. Neither metric alone measures muscle growth.

**Example:** 3 × 10 × 40 kg and 5 × 5 × 48 kg both total 1,200 kg of external load moved. They differ in sets, effort and recruitment conditions, so equal tonnage does not establish equal stimulus. Compare the same exercise with consistent technique and record RIR and recovery as well.

## Tempo and useful range: boundaries and exceptions {#effective-ranges-tempo-range}

The source separates eccentric, transition (amortization) and concentric phases. It describes broadly similar hypertrophy across controlled durations up to about 3 seconds per phase and about 9 seconds for a whole repetition, while allowing naturally slower near-failure repetitions. These are author interpretations of the evidence, not rigid speed limits. Pauses can improve control or alter loading requirements without being inherently more anabolic.

Useful range has three upper constraints: technical breakdown, extra motion that shifts demand away from the intended muscle, and painful or unsuitable joint positions. There are also deliberate exceptions to full-range defaults. A peak-hold row can emphasize scapular retractors in the upper portion; an advanced trainee can sometimes justify a partial when it clearly improves target limitation and SFR. These exceptions do not justify accidental shortening as load increases.

**Original comparison:** if one week’s press touches a consistent endpoint and the next stops several centimeters earlier, the heavier load is not an equivalent performance comparison. Record the changed technique before interpreting adaptation.

## Recap

- Load, effort and volume describe different dimensions.
- Landmarks move with recovery conditions and adaptation.
