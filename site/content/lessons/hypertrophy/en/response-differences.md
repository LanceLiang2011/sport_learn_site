# Why the same plan produces different responses

Individualization changes application, not the underlying need for sound principles.

## Sources of variation {#response-differences-factors}

Work capacity is the ability to perform work; recovery ability is the ability to restore function between exposures. They are related but not interchangeable. Anthropometry affects leverage and which muscles limit an exercise. Fiber characteristics, initial muscle size, adaptive response, age, training history, sleep, nutrition and life stress all influence useful training choices.

The book also discusses genetic influences on muscle shape and growth potential. They do not let a simple visual assessment predict a person’s final ceiling, nor can an exercise transform the underlying attachment anatomy. Different muscles in the same person can respond differently. Use group averages as starting hypotheses, then observe the individual.

## Learn from one person over time {#response-differences-feedback}

Individualize volume landmarks, exercise choice, progression, frequency and phase length by repeated feedback. A useful log records execution, load, reps, RIR, stimulus impressions, fatigue, sleep and relevant context. If the environment changes, prior landmarks are hypotheses rather than permanent facts.

Do not label every inconvenient exercise unsuitable for your body. New movements may need time for technique and awareness to develop. On the other hand, copying a professional’s plan or a training partner’s dose ignores their different recovery and experience. Make small informative changes so their effects can be interpreted.

## An original response-and-confound map {#response-differences-confounds}

| Observation | Possible contribution | Do not conclude automatically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| More repetitions across several sets | Work capacity, skill or fatigue differences | Faster between-session recovery |
| Fast early growth | Initial size, responsiveness or beginner status | The highest final growth ceiling |
| A different response in later years | Training age and changed circumstances | The newer method caused the entire difference |
| A muscle favors one exercise | Anatomy, execution and local response | The same exercise is best for every muscle |
| A sex-group average differs | Group-level traits and typical loading | Every individual needs the group-average schedule |

The source distinguishes initial muscle size, gain rate, response per unit stimulus and long-term potential. They are related but not interchangeable. A fair personal experiment also considers diet, sleep, stress and training-age changes. The authors discuss spending a block biased toward one repetition range while retaining some others, then comparing several indicators. Such an experiment remains observational; changing one emphasis does not perfectly isolate a cellular pathway.

## Recap

- Work capacity and recovery capacity are different.
- Repeated feedback refines general principles for a particular person.
