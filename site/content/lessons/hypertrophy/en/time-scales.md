# Make today support tomorrow

Phase potentiation applies from repetitions to a training career.

## Potentiation at several scales {#time-scales-scales}

Phase potentiation arranges training so that a phase both works now and improves later training. It also prevents adaptive decay: gains should not be unnecessarily lost while another goal is pursued. At small scales, warm-up repetitions and well-managed sets prepare later work. At larger scales, a technical phase can prepare harder loading or a maintenance phase can prepare another productive growth block.

Potentiation is not the claim that every preceding effort improves everything after it. A highly fatiguing trunk exercise before squats can do the opposite. Consider the target of the next exposure, the residual fatigue and which prior adaptations need maintenance.

## Leave a path for next week {#time-scales-progress}

The authors’ load-progression rule is to add only enough load that at least comparable repetitions remain possible at the same or slightly lower RIR. Their repetition rule mirrors this: add repetitions while retaining at least comparable load and the intended effort trajectory. The purpose is a productive sequence, often aiming for at least four accumulation weeks when appropriate.

Large equipment increments need not force an early failure week. Accumulate repetitions within a sensible range before taking the next load jump. An unchanged load with more repetitions at comparable technique can be progression. Conversely, adding too much weight can reduce future sets and shorten the growth phase.

## An original map across timescales {#time-scales-scale-table}

| Scale | Preparation to preserve | Cost to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repetition to repetition | Stable setup and practiced execution | A poorly controlled rep that disrupts the next |
| Exercise to exercise | Warmth, readiness or deliberate target pre-fatigue | Exhausting support muscles before the priority movement |
| Week to week | Enough overload and a viable next step | Large jumps that end accumulation early |
| Mesocycle to mesocycle | Useful skill and retained muscle | Carrying excessive fatigue into the next start |
| Block or career | Better limiting capacities and responsive training | Repeatedly rebuilding gains that were needlessly lost |

This table is an original synthesis of the source’s small- and large-scale examples. A deliberately targeted pre-exhaust is distinct from accidentally exhausting a support structure: the former aims to make the intended muscle limit the next movement, while the latter prevents it from receiving sufficient work.

## Recap

- Evaluate a phase by its present and future effects.
- Preserve comparable performance when increasing challenge.
