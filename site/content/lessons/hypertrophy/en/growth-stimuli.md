# What makes a set stimulative?

Mechanical tension, recruitment, effort and range of motion work together.

## Tension and motor-unit recruitment {#growth-stimuli-tension}

Overload has two components: a session must be challenging enough now, and the challenge must increase as adaptation changes capacity. This does not require lifting above a maximum. Mechanical tension is central: a motor unit combines a motor neuron with the muscle fibers it controls, and higher force demands generally recruit larger units under the size principle.

Heavier loads recruit demanding units directly; lighter loads can recruit more of them as fatigue builds near failure. A maximal single supplies high tension but little repeated exposure. Hypertrophy therefore depends on the combination of load, useful volume and effort, not on maximizing one variable while ignoring the others.

## Signals and proxies are not the same {#growth-stimuli-signals}

The source considers metabolite accumulation and cell swelling alongside tension as candidate contributors to growth, while recognizing uncertainty in their independent causal roles. Mind–muscle connection, pump and post-session disruption can help locate the stimulus, but none proves that a muscle will grow. Severe soreness can consume recovery resources and impair subsequent training.

Movement velocity and range of motion modify the stimulus. Controlled eccentrics, useful stretch and an appropriate contraction can train a broad range of motor units. Do not shorten range just to claim load progression. The source favors a full, safe, goal-appropriate range; forcing additional range that breaks technique or causes joint pain does not meet that criterion.

## Effective repetitions: a model, not a binary switch {#growth-stimuli-effective-reps}

The source calls repetitions near failure more effective because high-threshold units contribute a greater share of force, but explicitly notes that earlier repetitions are not useless. Counting only the last five repetitions as if all previous work had zero effect can produce misleading equivalences. Two submaximal hard sets and one failure set can contain a similar count of “effective reps” without being equivalent training.

Its stimulus diagrams show a threshold region, diminishing returns and eventually excessive-dose costs; their smooth curves are conceptual. They do not provide a measured equation for kilograms of new muscle. The soreness diagram makes the same distinction: some disturbance can accompany useful training, while severe damage competes with adaptation for recovery resources.

The authors’ proposed preparatory hypertrophy mechanisms include cellular fluid and glycogen changes, structural protein expansion and satellite-cell nuclear contribution before fuller contractile growth. This is presented as a hypothesis explaining why abrupt changes may lose some training momentum, not as a proven mandatory two-stage schedule.

## Recap

- Overload means an adequate and progressively challenging stimulus.
- Pump, burn and soreness are observations, not direct growth measurements.
