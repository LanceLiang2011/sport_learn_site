# Coordinate load, tempo and order

Variation must continue to serve specificity and overload.

## Match the method to the movement {#coordinate-variables-ranges}

The book organizes loading into roughly 5–10, 10–20 and 20–30 repetition regions. No region fits every exercise equally well. Very high-repetition loaded hip hinges may be limited by breathing or trunk endurance, while very heavy isolation work can shift force away from the target or irritate joints. It is unnecessary to force all ranges into every session.

Keep the same day’s exercise and range reasonably stable across accumulation for comparability. Controlled slower eccentrics or pauses can permit lower loads, improve technique or help reduce unwanted loading. They are not inherently superior growth tricks. Eccentric-accentuated loading is discussed as a potentially useful but uncertain tradeoff, with additional fatigue and implementation demands.

## Vary dose and order logically {#coordinate-variables-order}

Volume variation stays within current landmarks. A more axially fatiguing movement can receive fewer sets while a lower-fatigue complement receives more. Different loading ranges can be emphasized across days without replacing exercises weekly. Put priority muscles where they can receive quality work, and consider whether an early movement will fatigue a later movement’s limiting support structures.

Adding drop sets, myoreps or supersets is justified by their specific role, such as time efficiency or target limitation. Exotic cadences, constant novelty and inappropriate load–exercise pairings can undermine higher-priority principles. Beginners generally gain more from learning a small reliable exercise set than from sophisticated weekly rotations.

## Recap

- Use varied ranges while preserving repeatable progression.
- New methods must earn their place in the plan.
