# Concurrent activities and opportunity cost

Judge interference by overlap, stimulus type, energy cost and fatigue.

## Four questions about compatibility {#compatibility-overlap}

Ask whether an added activity uses the same structures, asks those structures to adapt in a different way, consumes substantial energy, or produces considerable damage and fatigue. More overlap with a conflicting stimulus generally increases interference with an exclusive hypertrophy goal. Long-distance running and quadriceps growth compete more directly than a short, easy recreational activity and arm growth.

The book ranks endurance and combat sports as less compatible with maximal hypertrophy than technique-based sports or low-impact hobbies. This is a goal-specific ordering, not a judgment of their value for health or enjoyment. Multiple goals are legitimate; account for their tradeoffs instead of expecting to maximize all of them simultaneously.

## Make tradeoffs visible {#compatibility-allocation}

Create a weekly map of muscle loading, demanding sessions, and genuine recovery days. A sport practice can load the same joints and supporting muscles as a gym session even when it is not counted as a lifting set. Energy expenditure also affects whether eating supports growth or turns an intended surplus into maintenance.

**Worked example:** a trainee wants larger legs while preparing for a distance race. Removing all running would violate the race goal. Instead, identify which goal has priority in the current block and reduce expectations or training demands for the other. This is an original decision example applying the book’s compatibility framework, not a quantified interference formula.

## Recap

- Other training consumes time, energy and recovery resources.
- Evaluate compatibility relative to stated priorities.
