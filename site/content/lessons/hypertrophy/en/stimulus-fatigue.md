# Evaluate stimulus per fatigue cost

A high stimulus is useful only in the context of its costs.

## What the SFR represents {#stimulus-fatigue-ratio}

The [stimulus-to-fatigue ratio](term:sfr) (SFR) is conceptually **hypertrophy stimulus ÷ fatigue generated**. The book estimates stimulus with challenge, pump and muscle disruption, and fatigue with joint/connective-tissue disruption, perceived exertion and effects on unused-muscle performance. These ratings are informal proxies; their ratio is not a laboratory growth measurement.

A chest-supported row may stimulate the back well while reducing trunk fatigue compared with an unsupported row. This does not make one version universally better: anthropometry, execution and the rest of the program change the comparison. Maximizing a ratio at a tiny dose also need not maximize total growth; enough useful total work is still necessary.

## Raise stimulus without needless spillover {#stimulus-fatigue-technique}

Stable setup, controlled transitions, suitable range and targeting the intended muscles can improve the tradeoff without simply adding weight. Avoid momentum that shifts work away from the target or extra axial loading that does not serve the goal. Compound and isolation exercises can both have high or low SFR, depending on the person and situation.

SFR generally worsens as very high volume or very low RIR creates disproportionately more fatigue. The source therefore balances effective work today with a second question: will this dose unnecessarily prevent planned progression later? A demanding set can be appropriate near the end of accumulation and inappropriate when it prematurely ends the whole sequence.

## Recap

- SFR is an informal comparison, not a physiological assay.
- Improve execution and exercise fit before automatically increasing dose.
