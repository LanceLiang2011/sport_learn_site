# Two clocks: recovery and growth

A useful model is not an exact biological stopwatch.

## Do not collapse the two time courses {#sra-curves-separate}

A session stimulates adaptation and generates fatigue. The [stimulus–recovery–adaptation](term:sra) (SRA) framework asks when another useful exposure can occur. Growth-related processes and restoration of performance are not the same curve; muscle can be stimulated to grow while fatigue remains, and a muscle that feels recovered is not necessarily still growing from the prior session.

The authors describe growth often occurring over roughly 1–3 days while recovery can take longer in trained lifters. This is a conceptual source range, not a personalized forecast. Tissue type, exercise, dose, effort and training history alter the time course. A diagram with smooth curves cannot identify an exact optimal hour.

## Frequency distributes dose {#sra-curves-frequency}

Weekly volume equals the sum of session volumes. Frequency is useful partly because it distributes hard sets into sessions that preserve stimulus quality. Twelve weekly sets in one session are not automatically equivalent to six sets on two days, especially if later sets in the long session become limited by technique, glycogen availability or systemic fatigue.

Functional overreaching may be planned at the end of accumulation if followed by recovery; it does not license permanent under-recovery. Evaluate both per-session and weekly limits, since raising the recoverable weekly volume of one muscle can still exceed whole-body recovery capacity.

> Scientific Principles of Hypertrophy Training, ch. 4, EPUB Text/part0013.xhtml, Per-week Volumes; Functional Overreaching; How Per Session and Weekly Volume Caps Impact Frequency

## Recap

- Recovery and adaptation have different time courses.
- Frequency is a way of distributing recoverable, useful work.
