# Sequence mesocycles

Changing emphasis is different from discarding every previous adaptation.

## Maintenance and resensitization {#mesocycle-sequence-volume}

Repeated high-volume mesocycles can increase required effective volume and work capacity while reducing sensitivity to a familiar dose. The book proposes lower-volume [maintenance phases](term:resensitization) to reduce fatigue and restore responsiveness. This differs from a short deload: maintaining muscle at MV for several weeks is a larger strategic change than a brief fatigue reduction.

The authors discuss approximately 3–6 weeks as a common maintenance/resensitization structure, while acknowledging uncertainty in exact durations and individual response. More is not automatically better; extending maintenance indefinitely also removes growth opportunities. Distinguish evidence, plausible mechanisms and the coach’s practical rule.

## Shift emphasis rather than exclude ranges {#mesocycle-sequence-bias}

An author example gradually changes the shares of sets in low, moderate and high repetition ranges: 50/35/15%, then 25/50/25%, then 15/35/50%. All three shares sum to 100%. This illustrates a bias shift across mesocycles; it is not a superior ratio proven for every lifter.

Retaining some exposure across ranges can avoid abrupt changes in fatigue and repeated recalibration. Heavier, lower-frequency emphasis may be followed by lighter, higher-frequency emphasis when appropriate, then a lower-stress phase. Exercise, cadence and supporting-muscle choices should fit each phase. The source warns against turning a nuanced sequence into 100% heavy, then 100% moderate, then 100% light work.

## Recap

- Deload and maintenance phases serve different timescales.
- Use gradual emphasis shifts; preserve useful exposures.
