# Compare stimulus, fatigue and time

The limiting resource changes which exercise is useful.

## Four comparison lenses {#compare-exercises-indices}

| Lens | Question |
| --- | --- |
| RSM | How much target stimulus does the exercise appear to provide? |
| SFR | How much stimulus for its fatigue cost? |
| STR | How much stimulus for the time spent? |
| SFTR | How do stimulus, fatigue and time costs combine? |

These are coaching comparison lenses, not precise biological units. When time is scarce, an efficient compound or superset may be attractive even if its fatigue cost is high. When a strong advanced lifter is recovery-limited, a supported or isolated movement may provide a better tradeoff. Compare the same person and target under reasonably similar conditions.

## Enough variety without fragmentation {#compare-exercises-number}

The source commonly suggests around **1–3 exercises per muscle per session** and **2–4 per week**, interpreted as broad author guidelines. Too many exercises repeat setup and warm-up work, leave very few useful sets for each and can exhaust available variation prematurely. A movement with full appropriate range already recruits much of the target muscle; every session need not cover every imaginable angle.

For example, two weekly sessions could share one reliable compound and each use one complementary isolation or alternate compound. Record which movement covers which action or regional emphasis. This is an original structure example, not a universal template or a promise that the listed exercise counts fit everyone.

## Recap

- Rank exercise tradeoffs relative to the current limiting resource.
- Avoid spending time on excessive setup instead of useful sets.
